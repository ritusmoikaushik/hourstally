If you're paid every other Friday, there's a fair chance you'll get 27 paychecks in one year instead of the usual 26. Which year is the interesting part, and it comes down to one bank holiday.

## Why a year sometimes has 27 paydays

Twenty-six two-week pay periods add up to 364 days. A year has 365, or 366 in a leap year. So every year your paydays slide one day earlier on the calendar, two after a leap year. Most of the time nobody notices. Then one year the first payday lands on the 1st of January, the slide has used up a whole spare fortnight, and that year fits 27 paydays instead of 26. For any one payroll it comes round about once every eleven years.

2027 is one of those years for some Friday payrolls. The 1st of January 2027 is a Friday, and so is the 31st of December. There are two "every other Friday" schedules in the world: one pays on the 1st of January, the 15th, the 29th and so on; the other pays on the 8th, the 22nd, and so on. The first one has 27 Fridays in 2027 to pay on. The second has 26.

So far, so simple. Here's the catch.

## The 1st of January is a bank holiday

Payroll is mostly direct deposit now, and direct deposit runs through the banking system, which shuts on New Year's Day. The Federal Reserve's 2027 holiday schedule lists Friday the 1st of January as a day with no ACH and no wire processing at all.

So that payday can't actually happen on the 1st. Payroll companies tell their customers to move it to the business day before. OnPay's own guidance for 2027 says exactly that: a check date of Friday the 1st of January should become Thursday the 31st of December, with the payroll submitted a couple of days earlier still.

Follow that through and the extra paycheck moves year:

- **On paper**, the schedule pays 27 times in 2027: the 1st of January, then every other Friday to the 31st of December.
- **In practice**, the 1st of January payday is paid on the 31st of December 2026. That leaves 26 paydays in 2027, and puts the 27th into **2026**, which already had 26 Fridays on that schedule.

And it isn't only a question of which day the money arrives. Wages count in the year they're paid. A paycheck dated the 31st of December 2026 belongs on your 2026 W-2, not your 2027 one. So for someone on this schedule, it's 2026 that ends up as the 27-paycheck year, on their tax forms as well as in their bank account. A calendar won't tell you that. Your employer's payroll will.

I'd put it like this: the calendar decides that one of these two years gets 27 paydays. A bank holiday decides which.

## Does it matter?

If you're paid by the hour, hardly at all. Each paycheck pays the hours in its two weeks, and an extra paycheck is just another two weeks of work, paid. Nothing is gained or lost.

If you're salaried, it can matter quite a bit, because your annual salary has to be split across the paychecks somehow, and there are two ways to do it.

**Divide by 26 anyway.** Every paycheck stays the same size and the 27th is a whole extra paycheck on top. Nice for you, expensive for the employer.

**Divide by 27 that year.** Your yearly salary stays exactly what your contract says, but every paycheck is smaller. EY's example uses a $50,000 salary: $1,923.08 a paycheck over 26, $1,851.85 over 27. That's about $71 less every two weeks, which is exactly the sort of thing that breaks a budget built around rent due twice a month.

Both are normal. Neither is wrong. The point is to know which one your employer uses **before** the year starts, not after the first smaller paycheck.

Three other things move with an extra paycheck, and they're worth a quick look:

- **Fixed deductions per paycheck.** If your health insurance or a savings deduction is a fixed amount every paycheck, it's taken 27 times instead of 26.
- **Retirement contributions.** If you set a fixed dollar amount per paycheck to reach a yearly target over 26 checks, the 27th check pushes you past it. Check how your plan handles that.
- **Budgets with "three-paycheck months".** With 26 biweekly paydays, there are two months a year with three paydays in them. Lots of people budget those as bonus months. The year with 27 has three.

## How to find out which schedule you're on

You need one date: any payday you've actually had.

The [biweekly time card calculator on HoursTally](https://hourstally.com/biweekly) has a payday calendar under the card. Put in the first day of any of your pay periods, pick the weekday you're paid on, and it lists every period and payday for the year, counts them, and marks the months with three paydays. You can step forward to 2027 and see whether your schedule is the 26 or the 27.

One honest note: the calendar counts paydays as they fall on the schedule. It doesn't move a payday off a bank holiday, because employers don't all do it the same way. So if your count says 27 for 2027 and your first payday is the 1st of January, read the section above. Your employer will probably pay that one on the 31st of December, and the 27 lands in 2026 instead.

If you run payroll for a small team, this is the week to check, not December. If you're salaried, a short email to whoever does payroll asking "are we on the 1st-of-January Friday schedule, and do we divide by 26 or 27?" will save you a surprise.
